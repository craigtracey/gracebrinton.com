Gut health

The Gut-Hormone Connection

Let’s explore how gut health affects hormones.

What does gut health have to do with my hormones?

Quite a bit actually! Our gut microbiome and our hormones have a bi-directional relationship, meaning they both affect each other.

A gut with healthy bacteria supports healthy hormone levels. And hormones such as estrogen are shown to have a protective effect on the lining of our gut, which helps prevent leaky gut.

If we have increased “bad” bacteria in our gut, this can promote inflammation, interfere with proper detoxification, and in turn negatively affect our hormone balance.

Additionally, if we have leaky gut (i.e. our gut is inflamed and damaged) we are not digesting or absorbing our food properly. This can lead to deficiencies in nutrients that our body needs to produce adequate hormones.

How do we assess our gut health?

In my free hormone guide, I discussed some basics for starting to assess your gut health. But today, let’s dig a little deeper. As mentioned in the guide, it’s important to start with some awareness.

Questions to ask yourself

  • How do you feel after meals?
  • How are your bowel movements? Do you go daily?
  • Do you get bloated, constipated, or have loose stools?
  • Do you have indigestion, like burping or heartburn?
  • Do you have food allergies or sensitivities?
  • Do you have skin issues like eczema or psoriasis?

Even if you don’t overtly feel you have any of these symptoms, unfortunately that doesn’t mean there isn’t underlying gut dysfunction.

Do you have PMS, hormonal acne, heavy bleeding, cramps, irregular cycles, hair loss (or excess hair growth), moodiness, fatigue, trouble sleeping, weight gain, or lack of appetite? These can all be connected to hormone imbalances with an underlying connection to the gut.

Common gut issues I see with my clients

  • Not enough of the good bacteria
  • Too much “bad” bacteria
  • Yeast overgrowth
  • Pathogens (like parasites)
  • Not enough digestive enzymes
  • Depleted immune system
  • Damage to the lining of our gut
  • Food sensitivities
 

If we want to support better hormone health, we must start with what is driving any imbalances. And that often begins in the gut.

There is a lot of general advice out there about supporting gut health. And that’s great. But it isn’t clear what is applicable to you personally.

Do you need more fermented food, or do you already have an overgrowth of bacteria? Do you need more stomach acid or digestive enzymes? How would you know the answers?
